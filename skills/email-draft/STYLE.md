# Northwind Studio: email style guide

The fictional style guide used as the evaluation target for this harness.
Northwind Studio is a small design agency that does not exist. The persona
and the rules are invented, but the shape is taken from a real skill: a
mixture of rules that are mechanical, rules that are approximate, and rules
that only a reader can judge.

That mixture is the point. A style guide made only of mechanical rules could
be checked with a regular expression and would not need an eval harness.

## Mechanical rules, exactly checkable

- Every client email ends with the signature block, exactly:
  `Northwind Studio | hello@northwind.example`
- Never use em dashes. Use a comma, a period, or a spaced hyphen.
- Never use ellipses.
- Label a hyperlink with the word HERE in capitals, for example
  "You can see the draft HERE."
- Write money as `$79`, `$2,000`, `$30/hr`. Never "79 dollars" or "79$".

## Approximate rules, checkable with a threshold

- Keep the body under about 150 words, excluding greeting and sign-off.
- Open with a time-of-day greeting and the recipient's first name,
  for example "Good Morning Dana,".
- Put "Let me know if you have any questions." on its own line immediately
  before the sign-off.
- Close with "Enjoy your day," by default.
- Quote work as hours first with the cost in parentheses, for example
  "about 20 hours ($800)", and say plainly that it is an estimate.

## Judgment calls, only a reader can assess

- The email gets to the point in the first sentence after the greeting.
  No throat-clearing and no summarizing back what the client already said.
- Requests are phrased as questions or as "Let me know if...", never as
  instructions.
- When a request would remove something the client already has, the reply
  presents the trade-off and hands the decision back rather than silently
  complying or refusing outright.
- The email never invents a specific. A price, a date, or a status that was
  not provided is marked as a gap in square brackets, for example
  `[confirm delivery date]`, rather than filled in with a plausible guess.

## Never write

These phrases are banned outright:

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "Please don't hesitate to reach out"
- "Best regards", "Kind regards", "Warm regards"
- Consultant register: "leverage", "streamline", "seamless", "utilize"
